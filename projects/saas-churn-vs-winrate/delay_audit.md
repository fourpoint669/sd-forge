A first-order delay releases its contents exponentially: with residence time tau, 39% leave before tau/2 (1 - e^-0.5) and 13.5% are still inside after 2 tau (e^-2). A real process with a roughly fixed duration (a contract term, a fixed review cycle) releases almost nothing before that duration; modelling it as first order makes responses start too early and spread too long.

## Judgment: real-world fixed delays modelled as first order (hand-written, not computed)

Percentages are the closed-form first-order release fraction 1 - e^(-t/tau) at the base time constant (computed with
python, not from a model run). "Fixed" means the real process has a minimum or near-constant duration.

| Stock (tau at base) | Real-world process (source of the time constant) | Fixed delay modelled as first order? | What first order implies at base |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firms in Evaluation (sales cycle time 6 months; capped by sales capacity) | B2B sales cycle (secondary benchmarks) | **Yes, flagged.** Minimum cycle duration. When the cap binds, closings run at the capacity rate and the pipeline queues (fixed-rate service), which first order does not describe either. | 15.4% of a cohort closes within 1 month, 39.3% within 3 months (when capacity does not bind). |
| Cooling Off Firms (cool off time 18 months) | Register: "Annual budget and contract cycles; no source". | **Yes, flagged.** | 39.3% re-enter within 9 months, 48.7% within 12 months. |
| Customers (1 / effective churn fraction: 150-200 months over the base run) | Logo churn 8%/yr, reduced by the CS programme | **Yes, flagged (judgment).** Churn is realised at contract renewal; the register does not state the term. The programme's churn reduction acts continuously here, so its timing advantage over a renewal-gated reality is overstated in the first year. | 7.7% of a new cohort churns within 12 months at base tau. |
| CS Capacity (CS ramp up time 4 months) | Register: "Hiring plus onboarding of CSMs; no source". | **Yes, flagged.** Hiring and onboarding have a minimum lead time; capacity does not start rising the month spend starts. | 22.1% of a funding step becomes capacity within 1 month. |
| Win Rate Capability (enablement ramp up time 6 months) | Register: "Training, content and tool adoption take quarters; no source". | **Yes, flagged.** Same reasoning: a minimum lead time. | 15.4% of the target capability within 1 month. |
| Recently Churned Firms (detractor memory time 12 months) | Fading memory of churned firms (referral effect; inactive at base, C = 0) | **No.** Exponential fading is a standard memory assumption, not a fixed duration. | n/a |
| Potential Customers (1 / adoption rate, 27-84 months over the base run) | Bass adoption hazard | **No.** Diffusion rate, not a delay. | n/a |

The order question in this model (churn first vs win rate first) depends on how fast each programme takes effect, so
the two ramp-up structures and the churn timing are the flags most likely to matter for the decision. That is a
judgment about direction only; its size is not computed here.
